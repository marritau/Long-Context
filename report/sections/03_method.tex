\section{Method}

For a question $q$, documents $D$, chunks $C=\{c_i\}_{i=1}^{n}$, and budget $B$,
PEARL builds an ordered evidence package $P$:

\[
P^* = \arg\max_P U(q,D,P)
\]

where utility combines relevance, evidence coverage, redundancy, position
utility, and token cost.

\subsection{Retrieval}

PEARL combines BM25, dense retrieval, reranking, and structural signals:

\[
R_i =
w_1 \hat{s}^{bm25}_i
+ w_2 \hat{s}^{dense}_i
+ w_3 \hat{s}^{ce}_i
+ w_4 \hat{s}^{struct}_i.
\]

\subsection{Evidence Graph}

Chunks form a graph with semantic, adjacency, and section edges:

\[
A_{ij} =
\eta_1 A_{ij}^{sem}
+ \eta_2 A_{ij}^{adj}
+ \eta_3 A_{ij}^{sec}.
\]

Personalized PageRank estimates bridge evidence:

\[
p^{(t+1)} = (1-\alpha)v + \alpha A^\top p^{(t)}.
\]

\subsection{Selection and Compression}

MMR selection balances relevance, graph score, and redundancy. Query-aware
compression keeps high-scoring sentences and their neighbors to protect bridge
facts.

\subsection{Position-aware Ordering}

PEARL models lost-in-the-middle risk with:

\[
u(p)=1-\rho\sin^\kappa(\pi p).
\]

The most important chunks are assigned to the highest-utility positions near
the beginning and end of the prompt.
